title: Trip plans and safety

A trip plan combines a launch site, vessel profile, planned date, and optional
departure time. It gives you a place to recheck readiness and share trip details;
it does not certify that the trip is safe or that someone will monitor you.

<Callout type="warn">
  Conditions change and forecasts are probabilistic. Before departure, verify
  current official weather and marine warnings, local closures and rules, and
  conditions at the launch site. A float plan is not a distress service.
</Callout>

## Create or edit a plan

1. Open **Plan a Trip**.
2. Choose a launch site. The form uses saved sites when available and can fall
   back to curated launch sites.
3. Choose a vessel profile. Its type, scoring profile, and custom thresholds
   affect the readiness view.
4. Select a date and optional launch time.
5. Review the preview and create the plan.

The form loads current conditions and an available forecast for the selected
site. The date follows that site's local calendar. You can edit an upcoming
plan; expired plans are not editable from the active-plan flow.

## Review readiness

The plan view shows a seven-day date panel when forecast rows are available. A
missing forecast stays unavailable; it does not borrow today's score. The
readiness preview can show a score/status, current signal readouts, a launch-time
strip, a typical on-water window, and solunar marks when those inputs exist.
These are planning details, not a guarantee that forecast data exists for every
future day.

Threshold bars visualize selected-vessel limits; they are not another safety
score. Choose a profile that matches the actual craft and your experience.
Recheck the current snapshot as departure approaches.

## Monitor plans

The trip list shows planned details, latest score when available, condition age,
and a **LAST CHECK / NEXT** summary. The server monitor runs every 30 minutes.
A status transition can create a Web Push or native-push delivery when a
subscription exists. Native delivery is not verified for a public store release.
The monitor may also send a pre-trip brief. Delivery acceptance does not prove
you saw a message or that conditions remain unchanged. This status-monitoring
path does not email the shore contact.

The list can export a CSV with date, time, site, vessel, stored status, and last
score. Past plans are separated from upcoming plans.

## Share a float plan

From an active trip, use **SHARE** to create a `/float-plan/[token]` link, then
send it only to the people who should know your plan. Anyone with the link can
see the shared launch site, vessel, planned departure, latest available
conditions check, score, and plan status without signing in. Revoke sharing when
it is no longer needed; revocation invalidates the link.

Treat the URL as a capability: do not post it publicly. The page is informational
and does not confirm that a shore contact is watching. A shared trip may include
a launch site that is otherwise private to your account.

## Configure a return check-in

On an active trip, set **TRIP CHECK-IN** to **ENABLED**, enter a shore contact
email and an **EXPECTED BACK BY** time, then save. When you return, choose
**I'M BACK — CHECK IN**. This is an acknowledgement workflow, not an emergency
response service.

If an active check-in remains unacknowledged past the return time plus a
two-hour grace period, the monitor can mark it overdue, send a push to the trip
owner, and attempt an email to the shore contact. Contact-email delivery is
environment-gated; do not assume it will arrive. If an escalation has already
happened, check in and call the contact directly. Disabling the check-in clears
the contact and return time; configure it again for another trip.

See [Alerts](/dontdiefishing/alerts) for notification sensitivity and delivery
limits, and [Safety & conditions](/dontdiefishing/safety-conditions) for data
sources and score limitations.
