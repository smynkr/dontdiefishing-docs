Manage your boats — photos, notes, maintenance logs, and document lockers in
DontDieFishing settings.

## Your vessel list

Open **Settings → Vessels** to see every boat in your account. Each vessel
shows its name, type, length, and scoring profile. The default vessel is
marked and listed first.

From the vessel list you can:

- **Add** a new vessel with its name, type, length, and scoring profile
- **Edit** an existing vessel's details
- **Set a default** vessel for scoring and trip planning
- **Delete** a vessel and all its associated data

Tap **Collection** on any vessel to expand its collection panel — photo,
notes, maintenance log, and document locker.

## Boat photo

Add a photo to identify your vessel at a glance. Thumbnails appear on the
vessel list next to each vessel's name.

- **Add or replace** — upload a JPEG, PNG, WebP, or HEIC image up to 10 MB
- **Remove** — deletes the photo from private storage

<Callout type="info">
  Photos are stored in private, owner-scoped storage and served via
  short-lived signed URLs — never public object links.
</Callout>

## Notes

Free-form notes per vessel for the details that matter on the water:
electronics, quirks, rigging, what to bring. Up to 4,000 characters.

## Maintenance log

Track service and repair history per vessel. Each entry records:

- **Kind** — service, repair, inspection, upgrade, or other
- **Title** — short description (up to 120 characters)
- **Date** — when the work was performed
- **Cost** — optional, in dollars
- **Notes** — optional details (up to 2,000 characters)

Entries can be added or deleted from the log.

## Document locker

Store reference files per vessel — guides, blueprints, registration,
plans, and more.

- Up to **20 files** per vessel
- Up to **20 MB** per file
- Accepted types: PDF, Word, Excel, PNG, JPEG, WebP
- Files open in a new tab via a short-lived signed URL
- Delete removes both the file and its metadata

<Callout type="warn">
  Documents are stored in private, owner-scoped storage. Only you can
  access your vessel files — there are no public object URLs.
</Callout>
