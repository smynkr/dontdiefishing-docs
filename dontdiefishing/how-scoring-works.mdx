DontDieFishing turns the available launch-site conditions into a 0–100 planning
score for a selected vessel. This page is the canonical explanation of the
shared scorer: what it reads, how it normalizes each signal, how your vessel
changes the inputs, and when a hard veto forces NO-GO.

## The scoring pipeline

For each conditions snapshot, the scorer follows this order:

1. **Select thresholds.** It starts with the default thresholds for the vessel
   type, then applies any custom threshold overrides.
2. **Select weights.** It starts with the chosen cautious, moderate, or
   experienced profile, then applies any custom weight overrides.
3. **Check hard vetoes.** A veto is evaluated before weighted scoring. If one
   fires, the result is immediately `100`, **NO-GO**, with a reason.
4. **Build signal rows.** Each signal records its raw value, normalized risk,
   availability, adjusted weight, and contribution.
5. **Normalize available weights.** Missing signals contribute no weight; the
   remaining available weights are redistributed proportionally.
6. **Combine and classify.** Contributions are summed from unrounded values,
   then the result is rounded, clamped to 0–100, and mapped to GO, CAUTION, or
   NO-GO.

## Conditions and signals

The web aggregator combines these sources before scoring:

| Signal or field | Source | Scoring behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wind speed and gusts | NWS forecast, with NDBC buoy fallback | Wind and gust risk are normalized separately; the higher risk is used. |
| Waves / swell | NDBC buoy | The scorer normalizes wave height. Wave period and swell direction remain raw condition fields. |
| Marine warnings | NWS marine alerts | Warning text is normalized by its highest recognized severity. |
| Visibility | NWS forecast | Lower visibility produces higher normalized risk. |
| Tidal current | NOAA CO-OPS current predictions | Current speed is normalized against the vessel threshold. |
| Lightning | NWS forecast | `none`, `low`, `moderate`, and `high` map to increasing risk. |
| Precipitation | NWS forecast | Probability is used as a percentage, clamped to 0–100. |
| Water quality | Snapshot field | The current aggregator returns `null`, so this row is unavailable unless another snapshot supplies a value. |
| UV index | EPA UV | UV is normalized against an index threshold of 8. |
| Water temperature | NOAA CO-OPS, with NDBC buoy fallback | Colder water produces higher normalized risk against the vessel threshold. |
| Travel time | Optional scorer input | If supplied, risk scales from 0 at 0 minutes to 100 at 120 minutes. |

Tide height and phase, sunrise and sunset, air temperature, wave period, and
swell direction can be shown as condition details. They are not weighted signal
rows in the shared scorer.

## Normalization

Every available signal is converted to a 0–100 risk value before weights are
applied. Lower values represent lower modeled risk.

### Threshold-based signals

Wind speed, gusts, wave height, and tidal current use a piecewise scale:

- 0 at the ideal value (0 for these signals)
- 70 at the selected vessel threshold
- 100 at 1.5 times that threshold

Values between those anchors are interpolated. Wind / gusts use the higher of
the two normalized values. Waves / swell uses wave height in the scorer; period
and direction are not additional normalized inputs.

Visibility and water temperature use an inverse scale:

- Visibility is 0 at 10 miles, 70 at the selected visibility threshold, and 100
  at half that threshold.
- Water temperature is 0 at 70°F, 70 at the selected water-temperature
  threshold, and 100 at half that threshold.

### Categorical and direct signals

| Signal | Normalization |
| --- | --- |
| Lightning | `none` = 0, `low` = 30, `moderate` = 70, `high` = 100. |
| Precipitation | Probability percentage, clamped to 0–100. |
| Water quality | `good` = 0, `fair` = 30, `poor` = 70, `advisory` = 100. |
| UV index | Piecewise scale from 0 at index 0 to 70 at index 8 and 100 at index 12. |
| Marine warnings | No warnings = 0; text containing `advisory` = 40, `watch` = 70, or `warning` = 100. Multiple warnings use the highest value; an unrecognized non-empty warning defaults to 40. |
| Travel time | Linear scale from 0 at 0 minutes to 100 at 120 minutes, capped at 100. |

## Vessel personalization

The vessel type controls the default environmental thresholds used by the
normalizers and veto checks. The defaults are:

| Vessel type | Wind (kts) | Gust (kts) | Waves (ft) | Visibility (mi) | Current (kts) | Water temperature (°F) | Lightning threshold |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Powerboat | 25 | 35 | 6 | 1 | 4 | 45 | Moderate |
| Sailboat | 30 | 40 | 8 | 1 | 4 | 45 | Moderate |
| Kayak | 15 | 20 | 2 | 0.5 | 2 | 55 | Low |
| Paddleboard | 15 | 20 | 2 | 0.5 | 2 | 55 | Low |

Custom thresholds are applied after these defaults. A vessel also selects one
of three weight profiles. The values below are the base weights before missing
signals are removed and the remaining weights are renormalized:

| Signal | Cautious | Moderate | Experienced |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Wind / gusts | 22% | 20% | 15% |
| Waves / swell | 20% | 18% | 14% |
| Marine warnings | 18% | 15% | 12% |
| Visibility | 12% | 12% | 12% |
| Tidal currents | 8% | 10% | 12% |
| Lightning | 8% | 8% | 8% |
| Precipitation | 4% | 5% | 7% |
| Water quality | 3% | 4% | 5% |
| UV index | 2% | 3% | 5% |
| Water temperature | 2% | 3% | 5% |
| Travel time | 1% | 2% | 5% |

Custom weights are applied after the profile defaults. The scorer then sums the
weights of available signals and divides each available base weight by that sum.
This keeps the available signals' adjusted weights proportional when a source
is missing.

## Hard vetoes

The scorer checks these conditions before it computes any weighted signal
contribution:

- Any marine warning containing **small craft advisory**, **hurricane**,
  **tropical storm**, **gale warning**, **storm warning**, **hurricane warning**,
  or **hurricane watch**.
- Lightning risk equal to **high**.
- Wind speed greater than 1.5 times the vessel's wind threshold.
- Gusts greater than 1.5 times the vessel's gust threshold.
- Wave height greater than 1.5 times the vessel's wave threshold.
- Visibility less than half the vessel's visibility threshold.
- Tidal current greater than 1.5 times the vessel's current threshold.
- Water temperature below 75% of the vessel's water-temperature threshold.

A veto result has:

- `total_score: 100`
- `status: "nogo"` (shown as **NO-GO**)
- `vetoed: true`
- `veto_reason` containing the matching warning or measurement
- an empty signal list, because the veto returns before signal rows are built

A warning shown in the UI is not automatically a veto. The marine-warning
callout displays any active string warning, notes that it may have changed, and
explicitly directs you to check official NWS guidance. Only the warning phrases
in the veto list force NO-GO.

## Status bands

When no veto fires, the final rounded score is classified as follows:

| Final score | Status | How to read it |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0–39 | **GO** | Lower modeled risk from the available inputs. |
| 40–59 | **CAUTION** | Elevated modeled risk; inspect the signal breakdown and verify conditions. |
| 60–100 | **NO-GO** | High modeled risk. |

A veto is NO-GO regardless of what a weighted score would have been. The score
is not a guarantee that a trip is safe, and GO is not an authorization to launch.

## Freshness, missing data, and fail-closed planning

The web conditions endpoint reuses a cached snapshot only when it is less than
30 minutes old. Without a fresh snapshot it calls the aggregator; if that fetch
fails, the endpoint returns an error rather than scoring an unknown fetch. The
aggregator records a `fetched_at` timestamp for each assembled snapshot, but the
shared scorer does not assign source-specific freshness ages: returned signal
rows currently have `is_stale: false`.

A missing signal is represented as unavailable. Its normalized value is 0 and
its adjusted weight is 0; the base weight is redistributed among available
signals. That is not proof that the missing condition is favorable. Treat a
missing, unverified, or unavailable input as unknown and fail closed for trip
planning: do not read an absent score as GO, and verify official weather and
marine-warning information before departure.
